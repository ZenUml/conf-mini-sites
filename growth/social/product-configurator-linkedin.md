# LinkedIn Post Draft — Product Configurator Scenario

Every solution-architecture doc I've ever reviewed has the same section: a wall of bullet lists, one per region × plan × user-count combination, trying to answer "if you pick X + Y + N users, here's what you get."

Nobody reads the whole wall. They hunt for the one row that matches their situation — or they just ask the sales engineer directly, which is exactly the conversation the doc was supposed to prevent. And every time the product changes, someone has to remember to update every row.

So while building Mini Sites, I tried something different: instead of writing out every combination, I embedded an actual configurator on the page. Prospects pick region, user count, plan tier, storage, and integrations — and the recommended architecture, estimated monthly cost, and feature availability update live, right there in Confluence. No spreadsheet, no "let me check with the team and get back to you."

It shifts the reader from "searching for their row" to "building their own answer" — which is a much stronger position for a sales engineer or solution architect to sell from.

This is the pattern I keep coming back to with Mini Sites: take the thing that's stuck as a static bullet list, and make it something people can actually configure, inline, where they're already reading.

If you write solution docs, sizing guides, or sales-engineering proposals in Confluence and you're tired of maintaining a bullet list per configuration — give Mini Sites a try on the Atlassian Marketplace.
