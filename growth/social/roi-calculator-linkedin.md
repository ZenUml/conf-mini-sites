# LinkedIn Post Draft — ROI Calculator Scenario

Every cloud migration proposal I've ever written had the same problem: the ROI math lived in a paragraph, or worse, in a spreadsheet linked three clicks away from the doc everyone was actually reading.

Nobody opens the spreadsheet. They skim the numbers you gave them and move on — even though the ROI section is supposed to be the most persuasive part of the whole proposal.

So while building Mini Sites, I tried something different: instead of writing "if your migration costs $150K and saves $180K/year, payback is under a year," I embedded an actual calculator on the page. Reviewers change the inputs — current infrastructure cost, migration cost, expected savings — and payback period, annual savings, and 3-year ROI recalculate live, right there in Confluence. No spreadsheet, no context switch, no "let me re-run the numbers and get back to you."

It's a small change, but it shifts the reader from "trusting your math" to "testing your math with their own assumptions" — which is a much stronger position to make a decision from.

This is the pattern I keep coming back to with Mini Sites: take the thing that's stuck as static prose or a linked file, and make it something people can actually use, inline, where they're already reading.

If you're writing business cases, proposals, or technical docs in Confluence and want your numbers to do more than sit there — give Mini Sites a try on the Atlassian Marketplace.
